Timeline

29 SEP 2025

Proclamation under Section 232

The U.S. President imposed national-security tariffs on timber, lumber and derivative products: 10% on softwood timber and lumber, and 25% on certain upholstered wooden products and on kitchen cabinets and vanities (including parts).

14 OCT 2025

Duties take effect

Applied to goods entered for consumption from 12:01 a.m. EDT. Coverage is worldwide, with caps for the UK (10%) and for EU and Japanese goods (combined with MFN, not above 15%).

STACKING

How it interacts with other duties

Covered wood products are exempt from the reciprocal IEEPA tariffs and the Canada/Mexico IEEPA tariffs, but Section 232 duties apply on top of any antidumping or countervailing duties. Drawback is available for these products.

31 DEC 2025

Scheduled increase deferred one year

A new proclamation postponed the 1 January 2026 step-up to 1 January 2027, keeping 25% on upholstered furniture and on cabinets & vanities through 2026 while negotiations continue.

1 JAN 2027

Scheduled: 30% upholstered, 50% cabinets & vanities

Unless revised, rates rise for origins without an agreement. Watch CBP's Cargo Systems Messaging Service (CSMS) for implementation guidance and HTS Chapter 99 numbers.

1 JAN 2028

Next Harmonized System edition

The WCO's next HS revision is scheduled to enter into force in 2028. Expect code changes across many chapters; check whether your Chapter 94 lines are affected before re-ordering in late 2027.

Market context

World

~US$178 billion

CSIL's early estimate of international furniture trade in 2024, after a 9% contraction in 2023. CSIL expected trade to decline in 2025 amid tariff uncertainty.

United States

US$41 billion

U.S. furniture imports in 2024 per CSIL — about a quarter of global imports. Residential furniture & bedding imports fell to about US$29.6B in 2025, down 12.7% (Home News Now).

India

~US$2.0 billion

India's Chapter 94 exports in FY2024-25, shipped to about 199 countries, with heading 94.03 the largest line.
