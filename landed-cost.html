Why this matters: with a 25% additional duty on an FOB-valued sofa, duty alone adds a quarter of your factory price — before freight, fees and inland delivery. On 1 January 2027 the scheduled U.S. step-up would raise that to 30% for upholstered wood furniture and 50% for cabinets and vanities.

Methodology

Duty base. The U.S. and Canada assess ad valorem duty on the transaction (roughly FOB) value; the EU, UK, India and most other countries use CIF — goods plus freight and insurance to the border. Switch the basis to match your destination.

Duties. Base duty, additional duty and AD/CVD are each applied to the duty base. In practice some measures do not stack (for example, U.S. Section 232 wood-product duties replace certain other additional tariffs); enter only the measures that apply to your entry.

U.S. fees. The Merchandise Processing Fee is 0.3464% of entered value, bounded by a minimum and maximum set annually. The Harbor Maintenance Fee is 0.125% of value for cargo entering through U.S. seaports.

Import VAT/GST. Calculated on CIF value plus duties, which is the standard base in the EU and UK. Registered businesses can usually reclaim it, so it affects cash flow more than margin.
