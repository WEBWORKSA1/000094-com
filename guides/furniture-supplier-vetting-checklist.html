Supplier vetting & compliance checklist

000094 Editorial · Updated 27 Oct 2026 · 9 min read

Most furniture import disasters are visible before the deposit is paid. This checklist turns supplier due diligence into a sequence you can complete in two to four weeks.

Stage 1 — Verify the business (desk research)

  • Business licence and registered address match the factory you are dealing with — trading companies are fine if disclosed.
  • Export history: ask for bills of lading or customs data showing shipments in your product category.
  • Bank account name matches the company on the contract. Never pay a personal or third-party account.
  • References from two buyers in your market, ideally similar order size.

Stage 2 — Audit the factory

  • On-site or third-party audit covering capacity, equipment, kiln-drying for solid wood, upholstery and foam handling, and finishing lines.
  • Social compliance: working hours, wages, safety and no forced labour. Retailers increasingly require recognised audit schemes.
  • Traceability for timber (species, origin, legality documentation); some markets require deforestation-free or legality due diligence.

Stage 3 — Samples and specifications

  • Approve a golden sample and keep a sealed counter-sample at the factory.
  • Write a spec sheet: dimensions with tolerances, materials, foam density, fabric rub count, finish, packaging and drop-test requirements, labels and barcodes.
  • Agree carton dimensions early — they drive your container load and freight cost.

Stage 4 — Product compliance by market

  • Upholstered furniture (U.S.): flammability labelling requirements and, where sold, California's TB 117-2013 smoulder standard and labelling.
  • Mattresses (U.S.): federal open-flame and cigarette-ignition standards with required testing and records.
  • Composite wood (U.S.): formaldehyde emission certification and labelling under TSCA Title VI.
  • Children's furniture and tip-over risk: stability requirements for clothing storage units; specific rules for cribs and children's products.
  • EU/UK: general product safety, REACH substance restrictions, fire safety rules for upholstered furniture in the UK, timber due diligence.
  • Lighting: electrical safety certification and marks — see our lighting guide.

Stage 5 — Inspect before you pay the balance

Book a pre-shipment inspection when at least 80% of goods are packed. Inspectors draw a random sample using ISO 2859-1 (AQL) tables — commonly General Inspection Level II with limits such as 0 critical, 2.5 major and 4.0 minor — and check workmanship, function, dimensions, packaging, labels and carton drop tests. Container loading supervision adds protection for high-value orders.

Stage 6 — Payment terms that protect you

  • 30% deposit / 70% against a passed inspection report and copy of the bill of lading is common.
  • Letters of credit or escrow for first orders above your comfort level.
  • Contract clauses for late delivery, quality failure, tariff pass-through and governing law.

Red flags

  • Prices far below market with no explanation.
  • Refusal of audits or inspections.
  • Changing bank details by email — call to verify on a known number.
  • Certificates in another company's name or with unverifiable lab numbers.
